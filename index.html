    Comparison

    Hen vs rooster

    Same species, different equipment. Use the Hen and Rooster switch on the plate above to compare the labeled parts.

    HenRooster
    Comb & wattlesSmaller and paler, brightening when she starts layingLarge and deep red, a signal of health and testosterone
    Hackle & saddleRounded feather tipsLong, pointed, glossy feathers that drape over the neck (hackle) and lower back (saddle)
    TailShort, fan-shaped, held fairly uprightLong curved sickle feathers arching over the main tail
    LegsSpurs small or absentSharp spurs that keep growing with age
    SizeLighter buildOften 25–40% heavier, with a more upright stance
    VoiceEgg song, clucks and the broody growlCrowing, food calls (tidbitting) and the loudest alarms
    InsideOnly the left ovary and oviduct develop. She stores calcium in special medullary bone for shells.Two testes inside the body, near the kidneys, not outside

    p. 2 · Field Guide

    Thirteen chickens. One species.

    Thousands of years of breeding turned one shy forest bird into hundreds of breeds. Here are some of the most distinctive, sketched as hens and as roosters.

    Feathers · structure

    Feather types

    Chickens don't have hair, but some breeds come close. Small changes to a feather's structure make it smooth, fluffy, fur-like or curled.

    Feathers · color

    Feather patterns

    Pigment is laid down in bands as each feather grows, so one feather can carry stripes, edges or spots.
